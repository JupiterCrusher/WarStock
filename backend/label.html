Label rules and examples

These labels describe what the text says. They do not verify events or predict future war.

  • Background: “Defense firm wins radar contract.” Talks without a new threat also belong here.
  • Threats / buildup: “Government threatens strikes after troop buildup.” A threat to attack is not a completed attack.
  • Reported fighting: “Airstrike hits military base.” Include attributed reports (“officials say”), but skip an unclear denial or dispute.

Use the main new claim. A ceasefire agreement that mentions old fighting is background. A new attack that mentions failed talks is reported fighting. Skip historical recaps, mixed claims, denials, and vague texts when you cannot choose from the supplied text. Don’t use facts found only in the linked article.

The first goal is 100 reviewed headlines. We’ll need more labels across several dates to train and test fairly.
